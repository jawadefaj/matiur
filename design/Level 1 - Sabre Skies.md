## 3. Gameplay: Sabre Skies (September 1965)

### 3.1 Gameplay Overview

**Genre:** top-down, vertical-scrolling jet action (portrait screen). The terrain scrolls down and the Sabre sits in the lower third.

**Setting:** PAF Base Peshawar and the skies over the Punjab border, September 1965.

**Core Concept:** The player flies three sorties of increasing difficulty, learning essential flying skills that will be needed in Level 2's low-level flight section.

### 3.2 Controls (Touch)

- **Drag anywhere:** steer (the jet follows the finger's movement, not its position).
- **Throttle slider** on the right edge: speed and fuel use.
- **Guns:** fire automatically when a target is inside the gunsight cone (option: hold-to-fire).
- **Pull button:** tighter turn, raises G.

### 3.3 Aircraft Systems

Kept small for a short level:

- **G-meter (0–7 G):** above 5.5 G for 2 s the screen greys and controls soften. 7 G for over 1 s is a blackout and the sortie restarts.
- **Fuel:** drains with throttle. Matters most in sortie 3.
- **Airframe damage:** 3 pips. Damage affects handling.
- **Wingman:** radio calls warn of threats and give context.

### 3.4 The Three Sorties

| # | Sortie | Time | Teaches |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Scramble.** Take off, join formation with the wingman, short patrol. Doubles as the tutorial. | 2–3 min | Steering, throttle, formation |
| 2 | **Intercept.** Stop intruders before they reach the base while protecting a slower friendly formation. | 3–4 min | Gunsight, G-management, priorities |
| 3 | **Low and home.** Damaged and low on fuel, fly back under 100 ft through valleys and over plains, then land. | 3–4 min | **Low-level flying, the exact skill needed in Level 2**; fuel and damage |

### 3.5 Tone Rules for Level 1

- No kill counter. Score comes from objectives (base protected, escort safe, route flown, aircraft landed).
- Hit enemy aircraft trail smoke and break off; they do not explode in a burst of debris.
- Enemy aircraft types should be period-accurate (Hunter, Gnat, Mystère, Vampire, Canberra) but not demonised. The level is about airmanship and duty, not national hatred. This matters especially for a Bangladeshi audience, given India's role in 1971.
- Do not claim specific missions or victories for Matiur until they are sourced.

### 3.6 Between Sorties

10–15 second in-engine beats at the flight line (crew chief, wingman, a letter from home), in the same style as the level videos.

## 4. Sortie Details

### 4.1 Sortie 1: Scramble (2–3 minutes)

**Objective:** Take off, form up with your wingman, complete a patrol.

**Teaching:** This is the tutorial sortie. Introduces steering, throttle, formation flying, and basic G-awareness.

**Flow:**
1. Start on the runway at Peshawar
2. Radio call: "Scramble! Bandits approaching. Get airborne."
3. Accelerate to takeoff speed, rotate, climb to altitude
4. Radio: "Sherdil Two, form on me"
5. Wingman joins formation from the left
6. Fly a patrol route while maintaining formation
7. No major combat; a few distant bogies appear on the scope but do not engage
8. Return to base and land to end the sortie

**Key Mechanics:**
- Demonstrate throttle control and fuel consumption
- Show formation flying and wingman position
- Introduce the G-meter by pulling hard turns (but not to blackout)

---

### 4.2 Sortie 2: Intercept (3–4 minutes)

**Objective:** Intercept and destroy enemy fighters before they reach the base. Protect a friendly formation of slower transport or bomber aircraft.

**Teaching:** Combat maneuvers, energy management, targeting, priorities, understanding the gunsight cone.

**Flow:**
1. Brief at the squadron: "An enemy formation is inbound. Friendlies at Angels 10 heading south. Clear them from the AO."
2. Take off and climb to intercept altitude
3. Enemy fighters appear: mix of Hunters, Gnats, possibly Vampires
4. Friendly aircraft are slower and undefended; they need protection
5. Engage enemies with guns (gunsight-based automatic fire)
6. After 3–4 enemy aircraft destroyed/scattered, friendlies are clear
7. Return and land

**Key Mechanics:**
- Gunsight cone and automatic weapons fire
- Manage G-forces during combat maneuvers
- Balance fuel consumption with engagement time
- If you let enemies reach the friendlies too close, the sortie restarts

---

### 4.3 Sortie 3: Low and Home (3–4 minutes)

**Objective:** Return to base at extremely low altitude, damaged, with limited fuel.

**Teaching:** This is the critical skill for Level 2. Flying consistently below 100 feet, managing fuel, and dealing with airframe damage that affects handling.

**Flow:**
1. Brief: "Mission success, but you've taken damage and fuel is low. Return to base at low level to avoid interception. Watch for terrain."
2. Start at altitude after completing a bombing run
3. Begin descent to below 100 feet
4. Fly a route home through valleys and over plains
5. Terrain hazards: hills, ridges, trees, power lines
6. If you climb above ~100 feet, a radar warning sounds (enemy radar is painting you)
7. If you stay too high too long, interceptors spawn
8. Landing at the base completes the sortie

**Key Mechanics:**
- Altitude band: 30–100 feet is safe; above 100 feet = radar warning → interceptors if you don't descend
- Terrain collision: hitting a hill, ridge, or power line restarts from the last checkpoint
- Fuel matters: if you run out, the jet glides down and crashes
- Damage effects: the damaged airframe is sluggish; the aircraft does not respond as crisply to input

**Why This Sortie Teaches Level 2:**
In Level 2, the player must fly the T-33 from Masroor toward the border at extremely low level to stay off radar. This sortie is a dress rehearsal for that exact challenge.

---

### 4.4 Sub-level: The Orange Raid (night, between Sortie 1 and Sortie 2)

**Type:** on-foot stealth (walk mechanic, map `orchard`, step `orange-raid`). About 3–5 minutes.

**Purpose:** a light, human moment between sorties, and a first lesson in the stealth rules Level 2 is built on (torch beams, cover, patrol timing), the way Sortie 3 rehearses Level 2's low flying. *Dramatised; not a sourced event.*

**Setup:** that night at Peshawar, Sherdil Two bets Matiur dinner that he can't bring back three oranges from the Station Commander's walled orchard without being seen.

**Map (top-down, night):** billets and the road at the south; the orchard inside a hedge, with a gate on the south side and a gap in the west hedge; rows of orange trees either side of a central path; the Station Commander's bungalow and verandah at the north.

**Watchers:**
- **Chowkidar Rahim** walks the west half of the orchard with a lantern (longest reach).
- **Gul the mali** sits by the east hedge, sweeping across the trees.
- **A sentry** on the verandah sweeps the north of the orchard.
- **Naik Sher Khan** patrols the road between the billets and the orchard.

**Goals:** pick the three ripe trees (PICK when next to one): west, in the chowkidar's round; east, near the mali; north, under the verandah. Then get back to the billet. Being seen fills the detection meter; full means "Halt! Who goes there?" and RETRY. Trees and hedges block the beams.

**Ending:** Sherdil Two: "Three! Not a whistle, not a lantern. How?" Matiur: "Keep low, keep to cover, wait for the gap. Same as flying."

## 5. Between-Sortie Beats

Each sortie ends with a ~10–15 second in-engine cutscene at the flight line:

- **After Sortie 1:** Crew chief debriefs; wingman calls on the radio.
- **After Sortie 2:** A letter arrives from home; a moment of connection before the final mission.
- **After Sortie 3:** You return victorious. Crew chief meets you on the tarmac.

These beats are styled like the opening video: dialogue is minimal, the camera pans slowly, the mood shifts subtly.

---

## 6. Level Archive Card

After Sortie 3, the level closes with an **archive card**: a styled frame with:
- A photo of the F-86 Sabre (or a period-accurate image)
- The date: September 1965
- A short historical paragraph about Matiur's role in the 1965 war and his move to instructing

The last frame of the archive card is the first frame of Level 2's opening video, creating a seamless transition.

## 8. Historical Notes

- The 1965 war lasted from August to September 1965.
- PAF Base Peshawar was home to No. 19 Squadron.
- The F-86F Sabre was the main PAF fighter of the era.
- Enemy types (Hunter, Gnat, Mystère, Vampire) represent Indian Air Force aircraft of the period.
- Matiur's specific sorties are not documented; Level 1 uses *representative* missions from the war.
- The game respects both Pakistani and Indian pilots; it is about duty and skill, not national rivalry.
